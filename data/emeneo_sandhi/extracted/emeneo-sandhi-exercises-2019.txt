2017PREFACE TO THE FIRST EDITION
Sanskrit has long been taught in conjunction with the elements of Indo-European historical grammar. Its potentialities as means of induction of students into methods of descriptive grammar have seldom been utilized in an explicit way. And this in spite of the honored priority and eminence of Pāṇini ( ? circa 5th century B.C.) as a descriptive grammarian William Dwight Whitney (1827-1894) as an arranger of Panini grammar for Western use. Perhaps the chief reason for this neglect is the fact that Whitney's Sanskrit Grammar is not in form a perfect beginner's book, even though his famous use of large and small print was intended to make it that. The device breaks down in chapter III, "Rules of euphonic combination," where much that even the beginner needs to know is given in small print and it is therefore impossible for the beginner in Sanskrit or for the non-Sanskritist descriptivist to find his way around with any ease. Nor do any of the professed beginner's books really make good this deficiency.
On occasion Leonard Bloomfield, to whose memory this pamphlet is dedicated, taught beginning Sanskrit in the Linguistic Institute. In the Institute of 1938 he handed over to me a class that he had begun and the notes that he had prepared for his own use. These included the selection of sandhi rules from Whitney's chapter III that he thought it necessary for a beginner to have an acquaintance with. Since that time I have, with his permission given initially, used and reworked these notes. They have been found so useful by myself and successive classes of students that it seems worthwhile to present them in printed form, along with a set of 27 exercises that I have constructed to give the student that familiarity with the rules that will fix them firmly with him. It is thought too that even a student of descriptive grammar could be given these exercises to work, without his knowing or learning any more Sanskrit than is provided in the pamphlet. The exercises do not proceed in the usual direction of those set, for example, by Nida, but to reconstruct from Pāṇini’s solutions the complicated data from which he started should give students practice in handling complicated data with exactness (which many of them fail in) and a feeling for the techniques of morphological analysis.
The particular style of statement used by Pāṇini, Whitney, and many other grammarians is one that is slightly out of fashion now. Yet it is a productive style and students in descriptive grammar should be taught to operate with it. Pāṇini set up for every polyallomorphic morpheme one of the allomorphs as a basic form (e.g., the verb root or the noun stem as basic to all forms of a complex verb or noun paradigm), and established a very complicated set of morphophonemic statements to describe the relations between the basic allomorphs and all allomorphs related to them. These morphophonemic statements are the sandhi rules of Whitney's chapter III and are stated in terms of process. Such statement is harmless if the student understands that it is descriptive process that is involved, and not historical. Consequently, Whitney's phraseology is not departed from very much in the sandhi rules here given; references are always given to Whitney (e.g.  HYPERLINK "https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Page:Sanskrit_Grammar_by_Whitney_p1.djvu/111" W241) and a violent departure from his words would have defeated the object of framing the essential set of rules.
The older readers of the pamphlet will recognize at once the influence of Bloomfield in the more Paninean and less Whitney-like overall arrangement and phrasing of the sandhi rules. Bloomfield was indeed an admirer of Pāṇini (others can vouch for this better than I), and all my changes of phrasing have not concealed this. But, thanks in large part to Bloomfield, we now all tend to approximate Pāṇini rather than Whitney in the style of our descriptive statements. In one major point Whitney has been left and Pāṇini's use of a morphophonemic symbol has been readopted, i.e., the use of ṝ in a number of verb roots (rule 39;  HYPERLINK "https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Page:Sanskrit_Grammar_by_Whitney_p1.djvu/111" W242a). Whitney's phonetic terms are now old-fashioned; the equations between his and mine will be self-evident. One transcriptional symbol differs from that used by Whitney; ś is now generally used in this country and elsewhere instead of ç for the palatal sibilant.

SANDHI
The rules that follow do not give all the sandhi statements that are made by Pāṇini and Whitney. However, in general, all other occurring combinations of phonemes than those dealt with in the rules remain unchanged, except for special statements that are given in the morphology rather than in the sandhi rules.
The rules are given in sequential order. A later rule cancels an earlier one. For every morphological situation not more than one pass through the rules is valid. The term "corresponding" means "phonetically closest."
Ниже приведены не все правила сандхи, которые перечислены у Панини и Витни. Однако, в целом, все остальные известные по правилам комбинации фонем остаются неизменными, за исключением определенных утверждений, которые приведены в разделе морфологии, а не в правилах сандхи.
Правила применяются именно в таком порядке. Более позднее правило отменяет ранее встретившийся. Для каждой морфологической операции разрешен лишь один проход. Термин «соответствующий» означает «фонетически наиболее близкий».
Internal Sandhi
This sandhi operates where suffixes are joined to roots, or to other suffixes; also where an item occurs in absolute final position.
Vowels
1. The succession of a or ā followed by a diphthong is replaced by one morphophoneme, the corresponding long diphthong ( HYPERLINK "https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Page%3ASanskrit_Grammar_by_Whitney_p1.djvu/71" W 127), e.g., deva- 'god' + āu 'nominative dual' ⇒ devāu 'two gods.'
1. При встрече a или ā с дифтонгом оба заменяются на одну морфофонему, соответствующим долгим дифтонгом ( HYPERLINK "https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Page%3ASanskrit_Grammar_by_Whitney_p1.djvu/71" W 127), например, deva- 'бог' + āu 'именительный падеж, двойственное число' ⇒ devāu 'оба бога.'
2. The succession of a or ā followed by an unlike simple vowel is replaced by a guṇa of the second vowel ( HYPERLINK "https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Page%3ASanskrit_Grammar_by_Whitney_p1.djvu/71" W 127), e.g., bhava- 'be + - ī- 'optative' + -t 'third singular' ⇒ bhavet 'he, she, it would be.'
2. The succession of a or ā followed by an unlike simple vowel is replaced by a guṇa of the second vowel ( HYPERLINK "https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Page%3ASanskrit_Grammar_by_Whitney_p1.djvu/71" W 127), e.g., bhava- 'be + - ī- 'optative' + -t 'third singular' ⇒ bhavet 'he, she, it would be.'
3. A diphthong before a vowel is replaced by short or long a (depending on whether it is a short or long diphthong) plus the corresponding semivowel ( HYPERLINK "https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Page%3ASanskrit_Grammar_by_Whitney_p1.djvu/73" W131). E.g., agni- 'fire'+guṇa+as 'nominative plural' ⇒ agne-as 'fires,' ⇒ agnayas 'fires'; nāu- 'ship' + ā 'instrumental singular' ⇒ nāvā 'with the ship'.
4. Final short or long i and u of a monosyllabic root or stem before a vowel are replaced by iy, uv respectively ( HYPERLINK "https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Page:Sanskrit_Grammar_by_Whitney_p1.djvu/72" W 129a). The rule also applies to the present-stem suffix -nu-. E.g., strī- 'woman' + ā ⇒ striyā 'by the woman'; bhū- 'earth' + ā ⇒bhuvā 'by the earth.' Perfect stems of roots in -u, - ū follow this rule (except bhū- 'become, be'), but those of roots in i, - ī follow the general rule (6), as do the Present and Imperative 3d plural of i- 'go.'
5. Final ṝ of a root or monosyllabic nominal stem (except nṛ- 'man'), after a labial consonant and before a vowel is replaced by ur; not before a vowel by ūr. After other consonants than labials and before a vowel, it is replaced by ir; not before a vowel by īr ( HYPERLINK "https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Page:Sanskrit_Grammar_by_Whitney_p1.djvu/111" W 242). E.g., pṝ- 'city' + ā ⇒ pūrā 'by the city'; pṝ- + bhyām 'instrumental dual' pūrbhyām 'by two cities.' kṝ- 'scatter' + ati '3d singular present' ⇒ kirati 'he scatters'; +yate '3d singular passive' ⇒ kīryate 'it is scattered.'
6. Short ṛ and other cases of short and long i and u than those covered by rule 4, before any vowel, are replaced by the corresponding semivowels ( HYPERLINK "https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Page:Sanskrit_Grammar_by_Whitney_p1.djvu/72" W 129). E.g., pitṛ- 'father' + os 'genitive dual' ⇒ pitros 'of two fathers'; juhu- 'sacrifice' + ati '3d plural present' ⇒ juhvati 'they sacrifice.'
7. The succession of like simple vowels is replaced by one morphophoneme, the corresponding long vowel ( HYPERLINK "https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Page%3ASanskrit_Grammar_by_Whitney_p1.djvu/71" W 126). E.g., a 'augment' + as- 'be' + īt 'third singular imperfect' ⇒ āsīt 'he was'; u + ucus ⇒ ūcus 'they spoke.'
Consonants
8.  At the end of a word, the cluster r + stop is not subject to the following rule (9), if the stop is part of the stem ( HYPERLINK "https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Page:Sanskrit_Grammar_by_Whitney_p1.djvu/80" W 150b). E.g. suhārd 'friend' + s 'nom. sg.' ⇒ suhārd ⇒ suhārt (31).
9. All but the first consonant of a final consonant cluster disappears at the end of a word ( HYPERLINK "https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Page:Sanskrit_Grammar_by_Whitney_p1.djvu/79" W 150) and is then subject to external sandhi. E.g., bharant- 'carrying' + s ⇒ bharan 'one who is carrying.'
10. Intervocalic ch is replaced by cch ( HYPERLINK "https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Page:Sanskrit_Grammar_by_Whitney_p1.djvu/106" W 227), i.e., ch is quantitatively equivalent to a consonant cluster; e.g., gach- 'go' + ati '3d sg.' ⇒ gacchati 'he goes.'
11. m before v or m is replaced by n ( HYPERLINK "https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Page:Sanskrit_Grammar_by_Whitney_p1.djvu/99" W 212a). E.g., agam- 'went' + va 'we two' ⇒ aganva 'we two went'; + ma 'we, more than two' ⇒ aganma 'we went.'
12. s between stops disappears ( HYPERLINK "https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Page:Sanskrit_Grammar_by_Whitney_p1.djvu/108" W 233c-f). E.g., acchid- 'chopped' + s-ta 'aorist 3d singular' ⇒ acchitta 'he chopped.'
13. s before a declensional suffix beginning with a consonant is replaced by ḥ ( HYPERLINK "https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Page:Sanskrit_Grammar_by_Whitney_p1.djvu/66" W 111a). E.g., havis- 'oblation' + su 'locative plural' ⇒ haviḥ + su ⇒ (36) ⇒ haviḥṣu 'in oblations.'
14. In certain forms, s before s is replaced by t ( HYPERLINK "https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Page:Sanskrit_Grammar_by_Whitney_p1.djvu/85" W 167). E.g., vas- 'dwell' + syati '3d sg. future' ⇒vatsyati 'he will dwell.'
15. s before dh is replaced by zero ( HYPERLINK "https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Page:Sanskrit_Grammar_by_Whitney_p1.djvu/84" W 166). E.g., śās- 'teach' + dhi 'imperative 2nd sg.' ⇒ śādhi 'teach.'
16. h in the root han- 'to slay' is replaced by gh when the vowel of the root disappears on account of a morphological rule ( HYPERLINK "https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Page:Sanskrit_Grammar_by_Whitney_p1.djvu/101" W 216l). E.g., han + anti ⇒ ghnanti 'they slay.'
Before zero, or when followed by any consonant other than a semivowel or nasal, the following statements (17 through 30) apply;
17. h in nah- 'to bind' is treated as dh ( HYPERLINK "https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Page:Sanskrit_Grammar_by_Whitney_p1.djvu/104" W 223g); e.g., nah- + syati ⇒ nadh- + syati⇒ (31, 33) natsyati 'he will bind.'
18. h in roots with initial d, in the noun uṣṇih- (a meter) and optionally in roots druh- 'hurt,' snih- 'be moist,' muh- 'be confused,’ is treated as gh ( HYPERLINK "https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Page:Sanskrit_Grammar_by_Whitney_p1.djvu/103" W 222a). E.g., duh- ’milk' + syati ⇒ dhokṣyati 'he will milk'; + dhi ⇒ dugdhi 'milk'.' + ta- 'past passive participle' ⇒ dugdha- 'which has been milked.'
19. In general, the following rules apply to h:
(a) the sequence of h plus t th dh is replaced by ḍh with lengthening of a preceding a, i, u; but vah- 'carry' and sah- 'bear' have o instead of lengthened a.
(b) h before any other relevant consonant, or before zero is replaced by k in verb forms, by ṭ in nominal forms.
E.g., lih- 'lick'+ ta ⇒ līḍha- 'licked,' but vah- ⇒ voḍha- 'carried, lih- + syati ⇒lekṣyati 'he will lick'; madhulih- 'bee' + su ⇒ madhuliṭsu 'in bees.'
20. kṣ in jakṣ- 'eat' is treated as gh ( HYPERLINK "https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Page:Sanskrit_Grammar_by_Whitney_p1.djvu/103" W 221a,  HYPERLINK "https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Page:Sanskrit_Grammar_by_Whitney_p1.djvu/109" 233f). E.g., jakṣ- + ta ⇒ jagdha- 'eaten.'
21. In general, the following rules apply to kṣ: ( HYPERLINK "https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Page:Sanskrit_Grammar_by_Whitney_p1.djvu/107" W 229b-c):
(a) before zero or s it is replaced by k in verb forms,
(b) by ṭ in nominal forms.
(c) before t it is replaced by s.
(d) before other relevant consonants, it is replaced by ṭ.
E.g., takṣ- 'fashion' + syati ⇒ takṣyati 'he will fashion'; + ta ⇒ taṣṭa- 'fashioned.'
22. Similarly, ch (the only examples are from prach- 'ask'). E.g., prakṣyati 'he will ask,' pṛṣṭvā 'having asked.'
23. Similarly, śc (the only examples are from vraśc- 'hew'  HYPERLINK "https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Page:Sanskrit_Grammar_by_Whitney_p1.djvu/103" W 221b). E.g., vrakṣyati 'he will cut,' vṛṣṭvā 'having cut.'
24. Similarly, ṣ ( HYPERLINK "https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Page:Sanskrit_Grammar_by_Whitney_p1.djvu/105" W 226). E.g., dviṣ- 'be hostile,' dvekṣyati 'he will hate,' dvit 'enemy nom. sg.', dviṣṭa- 'hated.'
25. Similarly, ś ( HYPERLINK "https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Page:Sanskrit_Grammar_by_Whitney_p1.djvu/101" W 218), but, in the roots diś- 'point,' dṛś- 'see,' mṛś- 'touch,' spṛś- 'touch,' and optionally in naś- 'be lost,' ś when final and in noun forms before s and bh, is treated as k ( HYPERLINK "https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Page:Sanskrit_Grammar_by_Whitney_p1.djvu/102" W 218a). E.g., viś- 'tribe,' viṭsu 'among tribes'; but diś- 'direction,' dikṣu 'in the regions.'
26. Similarly (to kṣ) j in the roots bhṛj- 'fry,' bhrāj- 'shine,' mṛj- 'wipe,' yaj- 'sacrifice,' rāj- 'rule,' sṛj- 'let go' and in the nominal stem parivrāj- 'wandering mendicant,' (but not in the nominals ṛtvij- 'priest' from yaj- and sraj- 'wreath' from sṛj-) E.g., yakṣyati 'he will sacrifice'; parivrāṭ 'mendicant, nom. sg.' mṛṣṭvā 'having rubbed,' but ṛtvikṣu 'among priests.'
27. jh is treated as k ( HYPERLINK "https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Page:Sanskrit_Grammar_by_Whitney_p1.djvu/102" W 220b; a grammarians' statement, without occurrences!).
28.  c is normally treated as k ( HYPERLINK "https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Page:Sanskrit_Grammar_by_Whitney_p1.djvu/101" W217). E.g. vac- 'say,'  vakṣyati, 'he will say'; ukta- 'which has been said.'
29. j is normally (i.e., apart from rule 26) treated as g, also before a few nasal suffixes (see exercise 15). E.g., yuj- 'join'; yokṣyati, 'he will join'; yukta- 'joined.'
30. t, th are replaced by the voiced aspirated dental stop after any voiced aspirated stop ( HYPERLINK "https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Page:Sanskrit_Grammar_by_Whitney_p1.djvu/83" W160). E.g., budh- 'be awakened' + ta- ⇒ buddha- 'awakened' (see 33); labh- 'receive' + tṛ 'agent' ⇒ labdhṛ- 'receiver.' But, the rule does not apply to the present stem dadh- from dhā- 'place' ( HYPERLINK "https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Page:Sanskrit_Grammar_by_Whitney_p1.djvu/276" W667-8) and to its desiderative.
31. Voiced stop before a voiceless consonant or zero is replaced by the corresponding unvoiced stop ( HYPERLINK "https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Page:Sanskrit_Grammar_by_Whitney_p1.djvu/77" W141,  HYPERLINK "https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Page:Sanskrit_Grammar_by_Whitney_p1.djvu/83" 159). E.g., vid- 'know,' vetsi 'you know'; budh- 'be awake' + syate ⇒ bhotsyate 'he will be awake.'
32. Voiceless stop before a voiced stop is replaced by the corresponding voiced stop ( HYPERLINK "https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Page:Sanskrit_Grammar_by_Whitney_p1.djvu/83" W159). E.g., śak- 'be able' + dhi 'imperative 2nd sg. aorist' ⇒ śagdhi 'help'; diś- 'region' + bhyas 'dative, ablative plural'⇒ digbhyas 'from regions.'
33. Aspirated stop before a stop, a sibilant or zero is replaced by the corresponding unaspirated stop ( HYPERLINK "https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Page:Sanskrit_Grammar_by_Whitney_p1.djvu/77" W141,  HYPERLINK "https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Page:Sanskrit_Grammar_by_Whitney_p1.djvu/81" 153). E.g., likh- 'paint,' citralikh + s ⇒ citralik 'painter of pictures,' budh- + syate ⇒ bhotsyate 'he will be awake.'
34. In certain roots, whenever the final aspirated sound is replaced by an unaspirated stop (by rule 33), the initial of the root (being a voiced unaspirated stop) is replaced by the corresponding aspirated stop, unless by an earlier rule (30), the final consonant of the root plus a consonant of the suffix result in a cluster ending in a voiced aspirated stop ( HYPERLINK "https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Page:Sanskrit_Grammar_by_Whitney_p1.djvu/81" W155). The roots are:
dah- 'burn (transitive)'	guh- 'conceal, hide'
dih- 'smear'	bandh- 'bind'
duh- 'milk'	bādh- 'harass'
druh- 'hurt'	budh- 'be awake'
dṛṃh- 'make firm'
and a few other roots in one or two forms only (note especially dh as initial reduplicating consonant in various forms of the verb dhā- 'place.') E.g., adhok 'he milked,' bhotsyate 'he will be awakened,' and dogdhi 'he milks.'
But certain forms occur with two aspirates; 2nd plural verb forms with middle voice suffixes, present indicative, imperfect indicative, present imperative; e.g., from duh- 'milk,' dhugdhve 'you pl. milk,' adhugdhvam 'you pl. milked.' Also instrumental
plurals of nouns, e.g., from budh- 'awake' ⇒ bhudbhis 'by those who are awake.'
35. A nasal before a sibilant is replaced by anusvāra ( HYPERLINK "https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Page:Sanskrit_Grammar_by_Whitney_p1.djvu/97" W204). E.g., man- 'think,' maṃsyate 'he will think.'
36. s after a vowel other than short or long a, or after k, r, or l is replaced by ṣ unless final or followed by r; anusvāra or visarga intervening between a vowel and s does not prevent the replacement, except in puṃs- 'man,' hiṃs- 'injure' and a few other words ( HYPERLINK "https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Page:Sanskrit_Grammar_by_Whitney_p1.djvu/89" W180-3). E.g., agni- 'fire' + su ⇒ agniṣu 'in the fires'; vāk- 'voice' + su⇒ vākṣu 'among voices.'
37. n, when preceded anywhere in the same word by r, ṣ, ṛ or ṝ, with no intervening palatal, retroflex or dental consonant (except y), and at the same time followed immediately by a vowel, semivowel or nasal, is replaced by ṇ ( HYPERLINK "https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Page:Sanskrit_Grammar_by_Whitney_p1.djvu/92" W 189). E.g., nara- 'man' + nām ⇒ narāṇām 'of men'; nṛ- 'man' + nām ⇒ nṝṇām 'of men'; brahman- 'priest' + ya⇒ brahmaṇya- 'pious.' (In a few words, ṇ occurs independent of this rule: puṇya- 'pure,' guṇa- 'virtue' and others).
38. A dental stop or nasal after a retroflex consonant (but not a stop after r-) is replaced by the corresponding retroflex ( HYPERLINK "https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Page:Sanskrit_Grammar_by_Whitney_p1.djvu/95" W197-8). E.g., dviṣ- 'hate' + ta dviṣṭa- 'hated'; ji- 'win' + snu 'agent' ⇒ jiṣṇu- 'victorious.'
39. A nasal before a stop is replaced by the nasal homorganic with the stop ( HYPERLINK "https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Page:Sanskrit_Grammar_by_Whitney_p1.djvu/99" W212). E.g., the nasal infix -n- is required for the present stem of yuj- 'join': yu-n-j + mas ⇒ yuñjmas 'we join'; yu-n-gdhi⇒ yuṉgdhi 'join.'
40. n after a palatal stop is replaced by the palatal nasal ( HYPERLINK "https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Page:Sanskrit_Grammar_by_Whitney_p1.djvu/96" W201). E.g., yaj- 'sacrifice' + na 'action suffix'⇒ yajña- 'a sacrifice.'
External Sandhi
This sandhi operates at the juncture of words in phrases and between members of a compound ( HYPERLINK "https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Page:Sanskrit_Grammar_by_Whitney_p1.djvu/65" W109b).
It operates also after declensional stems (other than ṝ- stems) before declensional suffixes beginning with bh and before su ( HYPERLINK "https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Page:Sanskrit_Grammar_by_Whitney_p1.djvu/66" W 111a), and often before secondary derivative suffixes other than y ( HYPERLINK "https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Page:Sanskrit_Grammar_by_Whitney_p1.djvu/66" W 111d).
Words are put into sentences and stems into compounds starting from the forms they have in absolute final position,
i.e., after completion of the internal sandhi rules. Consequently, the external sandhi statements cover only the vowels and 10 consonants.
Vowels
41. After the inflectional suffixes of the dual ending in -ī -ū -e, or after the nominative plural masculine pronoun amī, or after an interjection consisting of one vowel (e.g., ā), sandhi rules do not operate. This is called pragṛhiya ( HYPERLINK "https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Page:Sanskrit_Grammar_by_Whitney_p1.djvu/76" W138,  HYPERLINK "http://sanskritdocuments.org/learning_tools/ashtadhyayi/vyakhya/1/1.1.11.htm" P1.1.11-19). E.g., yajete ubhāu 'the two of them sacrifice.'
42. The succession of a or ā followed by a diphthong is replaced by one morphophoneme, the corresponding long diphthong ( HYPERLINK "https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Page:Sanskrit_Grammar_by_Whitney_p1.djvu/71" W127,  HYPERLINK "http://sanskritdocuments.org/learning_tools/ashtadhyayi/vyakhya/6/6.1.104.htm" P6.1.104); see rule 1. E.g., tatra eti ⇒ tatrāi 'ti 'there he goes.'
43. The succession of a or ā followed by an unlike simple vowel is replaced by the guṇa of the second vowel. Similar to rule 2 ( HYPERLINK "https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Page:Sanskrit_Grammar_by_Whitney_p1.djvu/71" W127,  HYPERLINK "http://sanskritdocuments.org/learning_tools/ashtadhyayi/vyakhya/6/6.1.87.htm" P6.1.87). E.g., tatra ihate 'he covets' ⇒ tatre 'hate 'there he covets'; tathā 'thus' uktam 'it was said' ⇒tatho 'ktam 'thus it was said'; brahma- 'priest' + rṣi- 'sage' ⇒ brahmarṣi- 'priest-sage.'
44. The succession of like simple vowels is replaced by one morphophoneme, the corresponding long vowel ( HYPERLINK "https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Page:Sanskrit_Grammar_by_Whitney_p1.djvu/71" W126,  HYPERLINK "http://sanskritdocuments.org/learning_tools/ashtadhyayi/vyakhya/6/6.1.101.htm" P6.1.101), see rule 7. E.g., tatra asti 'he is' ⇒tatrā 'sti 'there he is'; tathā aste 'he sits' ⇒ tathā 'ste 'thus he sits'; asti ilia 'here' ⇒ astī 'ha 'he is here.'
45. In all other cases, a simple vowel before an unlike vowel is replaced by the corresponding semivowel ( HYPERLINK "https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Page:Sanskrit_Grammar_by_Whitney_p1.djvu/72" W129,  HYPERLINK "http://sanskritdocuments.org/learning_tools/ashtadhyayi/vyakhya/6/6.1.77.htm" P6.1.77). E.g., asti atra 'here' ⇒ asty atra 'here he is'; iti 'thus' uktam ⇒ ity uktam 'thus it was said.'
46. After a short diphthong, initial a- is replaced by zero ( HYPERLINK "https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Page:Sanskrit_Grammar_by_Whitney_p1.djvu/75" W135,  HYPERLINK "http://sanskritdocuments.org/learning_tools/ashtadhyayi/vyakhya/6/6.1.109.htm" P6.1.109). E.g., vane 'in the forest' + asti 'he is' ⇒ vane 'sti 'he is in the forest.'
47. -e before any vowel other than a-, and -āi before any vowel are replaced by -a, -ā, respectively ( HYPERLINK "https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Page:Sanskrit_Grammar_by_Whitney_p1.djvu/74" W133,  HYPERLINK "http://sanskritdocuments.org/learning_tools/ashtadhyayi/vyakhya/6/6.1.78.htm" P6.1.78; 8.3.19). E.g., vane iha ⇒ 'in the forest here'; tasmāi atra ⇒ tasmā atra 'to him here.'
48. -o before any vowel other than a-, and -au before any vowel, are replaced by -av, -āv, respectively ( HYPERLINK "https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Page:Sanskrit_Grammar_by_Whitney_p1.djvu/75" W134b). E.g., tāu 'the two of them' atra + 'here' ⇒ tāv atra 'the two of them here.'
49. After a short vowel, the preposition ā and the adverb mā, ch is replaced by cch ( HYPERLINK "http://sanskritdocuments.org/learning_tools/ashtadhyayi/vyakhya/6/6.1.74.htm" P6.1.74); after long vowels, optionally. See rule 10. E.g., tava 'of you' + chāyā 'shadow' ⇒ tava cchāyā 'your shadow.1
Visarga-sandhi
(Visarga is the form in which -s and -r appear in absolute final position. For descriptive purposes it is expedient to operate with the original morphophonemes.)
50. The succession of -as and an initial a- is replaced by one morphophoneme, -o ( HYPERLINK "https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Page:Sanskrit_Grammar_by_Whitney_p1.djvu/87" W175a,  HYPERLINK "http://sanskritdocuments.org/learning_tools/ashtadhyayi/vyakhya/6/6.1.113.htm" P6.1.113). E.g., devas 'god' + asti ⇒ devo ’sti 'the god is.'
51. The -s of -as disappears before any other vowel than a- ( HYPERLINK "https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Page:Sanskrit_Grammar_by_Whitney_p1.djvu/87" W175c, P 8.3.17). E.g., devas āste ⇒ deva āste 'the god sits'; devas iha ⇒ deva iha 'the god here.'
52. The final -s of sas 'he, that one, nom. sg.' and eṣas 'this one, nom. sg.' disappears before any consonant ( HYPERLINK "https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Page:Sanskrit_Grammar_by_Whitney_p1.djvu/88" W176a,  HYPERLINK "http://sanskritdocuments.org/learning_tools/ashtadhyayi/vyakhya/6/6.1.132.htm" P6.1.132). E.g., sas gacchati 'goes' ⇒ sa gacchati 'he goes'; eṣas brāhmaṇaḥ ⇒ eṣa brāhmaṇaḥ 'this Brahman.'
53. In all other cases, -as or -aḥ (see rule 13) before a voiced consonant is replaced by -o ( HYPERLINK "https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Page:Sanskrit_Grammar_by_Whitney_p1.djvu/87" W175a, P 6.1.114). E.g., devas gacchati ⇒ devo gacchati 'the god goes'; manaḥ + bhis ⇒ manobhis 'by the minds.'
54. The -s of bhos 'O Sir,' disappears before a voiced sound ( HYPERLINK "https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Page:Sanskrit_Grammar_by_Whitney_p1.djvu/87" W174b, P8.3.17). E.g., bhos bhos deva ⇒bho bho deva 'Hail! Hail! O god'.'
55. The -s of -ās before a voiced sound disappears ( HYPERLINK "https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Page:Sanskrit_Grammar_by_Whitney_p1.djvu/88" W177). E.g., devās gacchanti 'they go' ⇒ devā gacchanti 'the gods are going'; devās āsate ⇒ devā āsate 'the gods are sitting.'
56. Before r-, final -s and -r disappear and if the preceding vowel is simple and short, it is lengthened ( HYPERLINK "https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Page:Sanskrit_Grammar_by_Whitney_p1.djvu/89" W179, P.8.3.15;  HYPERLINK "http://sanskritdocuments.org/learning_tools/ashtadhyayi/vyakhya/6/6.1.111.htm" 6.3.111). E.g., agniḥ 'fire' + rocate 'glows' ⇒ agnī rocate 'the fire glows'; punar rocate ⇒ punā rocate 'again it glows.'
57. In other instances, -s and -r final in a word after a vowel are replaced by visarga, and the following rules (58, 59) apply ( HYPERLINK "https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Page:Sanskrit_Grammar_by_Whitney_p1.djvu/86" W170a,  HYPERLINK "https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Page:Sanskrit_Grammar_by_Whitney_p1.djvu/89" 178a, P 8.3.15). E.g., agnis⇒ agniḥ 'fire, nom. sg.'; punar ⇒ punaḥ 'again.'
58. Any case of visarga before a voiced sound is replaced by -r ( HYPERLINK "https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Page:Sanskrit_Grammar_by_Whitney_p1.djvu/87" W174,  HYPERLINK "http://sanskritdocuments.org/learning_tools/ashtadhyayi/vyakhya/8/8.2.66.htm" P8.2.66). E.g., agniḥ asti ⇒agnir asti 'there is a fire'; punaḥ gacchati ⇒punar gacchati 'he goes again.'
59. Visarga before a voiceless palatal, retroflex or dental stop is replaced by the sibilant corresponding to the stop ( HYPERLINK "https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Page:Sanskrit_Grammar_by_Whitney_p1.djvu/86" W170c,  HYPERLINK "https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Page:Sanskrit_Grammar_by_Whitney_p1.djvu/89" W178a,  HYPERLINK "http://sanskritdocuments.org/learning_tools/ashtadhyayi/vyakhya/8/8.3.38.htm" P8.3.38,  HYPERLINK "http://sanskritdocuments.org/learning_tools/ashtadhyayi/vyakhya/8/8.3.39.htm" P8.3.39). E.g., tataḥ 'then' + ca 'and' ⇒tataś ca 'and then'; cakṣuḥ 'eye' + te 'your' ⇒ cakṣus te 'your eye'; punaḥ ca ⇒punaś ca 'and again.'
Nasals
60. -m before a consonant is replaced by anusvāra ( HYPERLINK "https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Page:Sanskrit_Grammar_by_Whitney_p1.djvu/99" W213i,  HYPERLINK "http://sanskritdocuments.org/learning_tools/ashtadhyayi/vyakhya/8/8.3.23.htm" P8.3.23). E.g., devam 'god, accusative case' paśyati 'he sees' ⇒ devaṃ paśyati 'he sees the god.'
61. Final velar, retroflex, or dental nasal after a short simple vowel and before a vowel, is doubled ( HYPERLINK "https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Page:Sanskrit_Grammar_by_Whitney_p1.djvu/98" W210,  HYPERLINK "http://sanskritdocuments.org/learning_tools/ashtadhyayi/vyakhya/8/8.3.32.htm" P8.3.32). E.g., hasan 'smiling' + agacchat 'he went'⇒ hasann agacchat 'he went smiling.'
62. Final -n before a voiceless palatal, retroflex or dental stop is replaced by anusvāra plus the sibilant homorganic with the stop ( HYPERLINK "https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Page:Sanskrit_Grammar_by_Whitney_p1.djvu/98" W208,  HYPERLINK "http://sanskritdocuments.org/learning_tools/ashtadhyayi/vyakhya/8/8.3.7.htm" P8.3.7). E.g., aśvān 'horses, acc. pl.' + corayati 'he steals' ⇒ aśvāṃś corayati 'he steals horses'; bhavān 'the respected person' + tvarati 'hurries' ⇒ bhavāṃs tvarati 'your honor is hurrying.'
63. Final -n before a voiced palatal, retroflex, or dental stop is replaced by the nasal homorganic with the stop (ñ, ṇ) ( HYPERLINK "https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Page:Sanskrit_Grammar_by_Whitney_p1.djvu/96" W202b,  HYPERLINK "https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Page:Sanskrit_Grammar_by_Whitney_p1.djvu/97" W205b,  HYPERLINK "http://sanskritdocuments.org/learning_tools/ashtadhyayi/vyakhya/8/8.3.24.htm" P8.3.24,  HYPERLINK "http://sanskritdocuments.org/learning_tools/ashtadhyayi/vyakhya/8/8.4.59.htm" P8.4.59). E.g., devān 'gods, acc. pl.' jayati 'he conquers' ⇒ devāñ javati 'he conquers the gods.'
64. The succession -n ś- is replaced by -ñ ch- ( HYPERLINK "https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Page:Sanskrit_Grammar_by_Whitney_p1.djvu/96" W203,  HYPERLINK "http://sanskritdocuments.org/learning_tools/ashtadhyayi/vyakhya/8/8.3.31.htm" P8.3.31 etc.). E.g., devān śṛṇoti 'he hears'⇒ devāñ chṛṇoti 'he hears the gods.'
65. Final -n before l- is replaced by anusvāra plus l ( HYPERLINK "https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Page:Sanskrit_Grammar_by_Whitney_p1.djvu/97" W206). E.g., aśvān labhate 'he receives'⇒ aśvāṃl labhate 'he receives horses.'
Stops
66. After -t, ś- is replaced by ch- ( HYPERLINK "https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Page:Sanskrit_Grammar_by_Whitney_p1.djvu/96" W203,  HYPERLINK "http://sanskritdocuments.org/learning_tools/ashtadhyayi/vyakhya/8/8.4.63.htm" P8.4.63 etc.). E.g., tat śṛṇoti ⇒ tat chṛṇoti ⇒ (67) tac chṛṇoti 'he hears it.'

E.g., tat ca⇒ tac ca 'and that'; tat + ṭīkā ⇒ taṭṭīkā- 'commentary on that'; tat jalam 'water' ⇒ taj jalam 'that water.' See rule 71.
68. -t before l- is replaced by l ( HYPERLINK "https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Page:Sanskrit_Grammar_by_Whitney_p1.djvu/84" W162,  HYPERLINK "http://sanskritdocuments.org/learning_tools/ashtadhyayi/vyakhya/8/8.4.60.htm" P8.4.60). E.g., tat labhate ⇒ tal labhate 'he receives that.'
69. A stop before a nasal is replaced by the nasal in the position of the stop ( HYPERLINK "https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Page:Sanskrit_Grammar_by_Whitney_p1.djvu/83" W161,  HYPERLINK "http://sanskritdocuments.org/learning_tools/ashtadhyayi/vyakhya/8/8.4.45.htm" P8.4.45). E.g. vāk 'voice' + mama 'mine' ⇒ vāñ mama 'my voice'; tat mitram 'friend' ⇒ tan mitram 'that friend.'
70. After a stop, h- is replaced by the voiced aspirated stop homorganic with the original final stop ( HYPERLINK "https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Page:Sanskrit_Grammar_by_Whitney_p1.djvu/84" W163,  HYPERLINK "http://sanskritdocuments.org/learning_tools/ashtadhyayi/vyakhya/8/8.4.62.htm" P8.4.62). E.g., tat hirapyam 'gold' ⇒ tat dhirapyam ⇒ tad dhiranyam 'that gold.'
71. Any voiceless stop before a voiced sound is replaced by the corresponding (unaspirated) voiced stop ( HYPERLINK "https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Page:Sanskrit_Grammar_by_Whitney_p1.djvu/82" W157c,  HYPERLINK "https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Page:Sanskrit_Grammar_by_Whitney_p1.djvu/83" W159,  HYPERLINK "http://sanskritdocuments.org/learning_tools/ashtadhyayi/vyakhya/8/8.2.39.htm" P8.2.39). Reduplication and Morphology
in Exercises 16-18 and 21-23
In exercises 16-18, the forms have initial reduplication. Reduplication is the repetition of the root syllable. The first syllable is called the reduplicate. The following changes apply to the reduplicate.
Consonants
. No final consonants are retained.
2. Of an initial sibilant + stop cluster, only the second one is retained.
3. Of other initial consonant clusters, the first one only remains.
4. A velar or h is replaced by the corresponding palatal (k by c, g by j).
5. Aspiration is lost.
Vowels
6. ṛ is replaced by a.
7. Long simple vowels are replaced by short ones.
In exercises 16 and 17, in the third singular active the root syllable shows:
(1) vṛddhi if it has a final vowel or followed by a single consonant (ch is not a single consonant).
(2) guṇa if the vowel is i, u, or ṛ followed by a consonant
(3) otherwise no change.
In these two exercises, all other forms than the third singular active in general show the root syllable with vowel unchanged (except for the general sandhi changes, e.g., final ū > uv before a vowel according to rule 4).
But, the third plural in exercise 16, numbers 6, 8, and 11, loses the vowel s of the root syllable, and in exercise 16, numbers-9, 13, 16, and 19, has no reduplication and replaces a by e.
