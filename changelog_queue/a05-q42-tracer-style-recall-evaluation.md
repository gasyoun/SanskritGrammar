Net-new generator [`scripts/q42_tracer_style_recall_evaluation.py`](https://github.com/gasyoun/SanskritGrammar/blob/main/scripts/q42_tracer_style_recall_evaluation.py) implements the one transferable finding from Miyagawa et al. 2024 (NLP4DH, TRACER validated *on Sanskrit*): smaller matching chunks raise recall over whole-string comparison — 4-gram shingle-seeded candidate generation (the shape both TRACER's anchor n-grams and Passim's MinHash/LSH reduce to) over the same 3-book pool (Bühler/Knauer/Kochergina) the 124-cluster catalog was built from, then sliding-window `SequenceMatcher` scoring at chunk sizes 8/12/16/20 chars vs a whole-string reference row. **Result:** the shingle-seeding step recovers 76/78 (97.4%) of the existing 128-pair gold's true pairs (2 misses are the same transposition edit, a documented blind spot of contiguous-shingle seeding, not of the chunk scorer). Beyond that regression floor, smaller chunks surface pairs the shipped whole-sentence 0.82 gate never sees at all: 168 new candidates at W=8, 35 at W=12, 6 at W=16, 7 at W=20, 0 at whole-string — confirming the paper's finding on this corpus. **Scope, stated explicitly, not silently dropped:** the 216 new candidates are UNVERIFIED — no H327-style human review pass has scored them, so this reports a discovery count and score distribution, not a verified recall percentage (the same single-annotator-overclaim discipline the roadmap's own Q3.3 gate already enforces elsewhere). A verified Δ recall number needs that review pass run on the new-candidate pool first — named as the owner residual.
