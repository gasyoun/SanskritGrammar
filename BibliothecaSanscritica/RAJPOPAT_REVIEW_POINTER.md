**Primary record:** Peter M. Scharf, *Review of Rishi Rajpopat, "In Pāṇini we trust. Discovering the algorithm for rule conflict resolution in the Aṣṭādhyāyī." Ph.D. dissertation, University of Cambridge, 2021.* Published by The Sanskrit Library, 23 December 2022, 83 pp. Free PDF on the SL publications page; a copy is landed in the literature corpus: [SanskritLexicography/literature/scharf-ReviewOfRajpopat-InPaniniWeTrust.pdf](https://github.com/gasyoun/SanskritLexicography/blob/master/literature/scharf-ReviewOfRajpopat-InPaniniWeTrust.pdf) (SHA-256 `6ed2b023…7fe7f3`, full hash in the [rights note](https://github.com/gasyoun/SanskritLexicography/blob/master/literature/SANSKRITLIBRARY_PDFS_RIGHTS_NOTE_26-09-2026.md)).

**In one line (from the review's own first pages):** the review grants Rajpopat's affix-before-base ordering observation (and his trayaṇām/7.1.53 discovery process) but argues, with derivational counterexamples (*bhavya* from √as, *bhavanti*), that reading A. 1.4.2 *vipratiṣedhe param kāryam* as a universal "right-hand operation first" algorithm does not select the correct operation in all cases; the Pāṇinian metarule *varṇād aṅgaṃ balīyaḥ* (PBIS 56) is the simpler precedence account.

**Why it matters here:** rule-conflict resolution and operation ordering (DOI) are exactly the mechanics any computational implementation of Pāṇinian derivation in the estate's grammar lane must decide explicitly — a citation anchor for prakriyā discussions in SanskritGrammar.
